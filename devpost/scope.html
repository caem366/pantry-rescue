Pantry Rescue

Meal planning that starts with your kitchen, not an empty shopping cart.

Inventory-firstCalmly agenticCart ready for review

The unique kernel

Turn a real, time-sensitive kitchen inventory into a practical meal plan—then visibly shop only the gaps. The user can follow every link in the chain, from what they own to a review-ready cart.

Who it is for

Young adults living alone or with roommates who have food at home, but don’t know what to make before it spoils. They want to spend less and waste less without turning meal planning into a chore.

The one story the demo must prove

1. Kitchen inventory
→
2. Rescue urgent food
→
3. Practical meal plan
→
4. Identify gaps
→
5. Agent builds cart

“Oh, that’s cool” moment

Manually add one or two groceries, load the realistic sample pantry, then reveal a plan that uses 17 of 23 needed ingredients and rescues food nearing expiry. The visible agent creates a cart containing only the six missing items.

Product identity

Polished and quietly satisfying: users feel financially clever and calmly guided—“I already had most of what I needed.” Rescue signals celebrate useful outcomes without lecturing or shaming.

Why this project

A chance to practice product planning, AI-agent workflows, and a working web app while demonstrating an agent that takes transparent, bounded action rather than generating another list.

POC boundary

Build now
  • Manual pantry input: name, quantity, optional use-by/urgency.
  • Realistic preloaded pantry for a repeatable demo.
  • Meal plan that favors expiring food and honors limited constraints.
  • Explanations of ingredients used/rescued, missing items, and a Rescue Score or equivalent.
  • One supported retailer path, with unsupported retailers clearly labelled.
  • Functional, visible shopping agent: searches, choices, quantities, progress, and cart derive from actual plan gaps.
  • A controlled storefront may substitute for fragile live retailer automation.
Later
  • More live retailer automations and household modes.
  • Photo pantry recognition.
  • Expanded nutrition, macro, and budget planning.
  • Checkout, payment, delivery, and account integrations.
Explicitly cut
  • Universal retailer support—unreliable automation would weaken the demo.
  • Automatic checkout/payment—unsafe and outside the proof.
  • A full nutrition product—constraints support the core, not replace it.
  • A generic recipe chatbot—it cannot prove the inventory-to-cart loop.
